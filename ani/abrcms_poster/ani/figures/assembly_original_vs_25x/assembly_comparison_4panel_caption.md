# Figure caption

**Effects of Sequencing-Read Downsampling on Genomic Similarity and Assembly Contiguity.** Original full-read assemblies (muted navy) and assemblies generated after nominal 25× read downsampling and reassembly (teal) are shown for six paired isolate labels (n = 6). Each pair was compared with the same selected reference, held fixed between conditions for that isolate. (A) Forward average nucleotide identity (ANI, assembly → reference; 90–100% axis). (B) Forward alignment fraction (AF, assembly → reference) expressed as a percentage. (C) Assembly contig count on a logarithmic axis. (D) Assembly N50 in kilobases on a logarithmic axis. Thin gray lines connect the two assemblies associated with each isolate; they are visual guides, not fitted trends. Values come directly from `ani/abrcms_poster/ani/exact_depth_validation_20261112/metrics/exact_depth_comparison_v2.tsv`, using `ani_forward_pct`, `af_forward × 100`, `contigs`, and `n50_bp / 1,000`.

The original and downsampled assemblies derive from the same sequencing runs and are paired observations, not independent biological replicates. Reference selection was based on the 25× comparisons, so ANI and AF depend on that selected reference and should not be interpreted as unbiased species assignments. These assembly-level differences do not establish that downsampling removed contaminants or that 25× is optimal across isolates. No significance test was performed.
